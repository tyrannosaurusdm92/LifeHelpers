# Mandarin: Old Chinese, Sinitic siblings and Han writing
Late Shang oracle-bone records attest Old Chinese c.1250–1045 BCE. This dates texts/artifacts, not speech origins. Sinitic sibling groupings commonly include Jin, Wu, Gan, Xiang, Hakka, Yue, Min, Hui and Pinghua; classification counts vary. Middle Chinese evidence preserves richer codas and tone categories; 1324 Zhongyuan Yinyun documents Old Mandarin. PRC adopted Pinyin in 1958.

Chinese script history includes oracle bone, bronze, seal, clerical, regular, running and cursive forms. Han characters are morphosyllabic; Pinyin is an auxiliary Romanization. Simplified and traditional forms coexist. Unicode Han main block U+4E00–U+9FFF. Pinyin ā U+0101, á U+00E1, ǎ U+01CE, à U+00E0; combining tone marks U+0304/0301/030C/0300; ü U+00FC. Stroke order is not encoded; no native subscripts.

Modern syllable template roughly (C)(G)V(X), with nasal codas and four lexical tones plus neutral tone. Mandarin lost Middle Chinese final /p t k/, redistributing entering-tone items. Earlier onset voicing conditioned tone splits across Sinitic. Modern Mandarin is broadly SVO/topic-comment; particles, aspect, classifiers and coverbs are key.
