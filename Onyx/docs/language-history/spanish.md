# Spanish: Latin descent, Romance siblings and spelling
Latin inscriptions attest the ancestor by the 7th–6th c. BCE. Spanish later developed from regional spoken Latin over centuries. Romance siblings include Portuguese/Galician, Astur-Leonese, Aragonese, Catalan (classification debated), French, Italian, Sardinian and Romanian. Latin’s case endings eroded; prepositions, clitics and word order grew in importance.

Latin script reached Rome through Etruscan adaptation of Greek. Ancient capitals, book hands, cursive and medieval minuscule differ in glyph and stroke practice. Ñ is a letter; ch/ll are digraphs. Unicode: ñ U+00F1, Ñ U+00D1; acute vowels U+00E1, E9, ED, F3, FA; ü U+00FC; ¿ U+00BF and ¡ U+00A1. Accents may be precomposed or decomposed combining sequences. No native subscripts.

Spanish has five vowels; common syllable form (C)(C)V(C), stress rules depend on word ending and accents. SVO is common but order is flexible and subjects may be omitted. Castilian initial Latin f often developed via h to zero (farina > harina, filium > hijo); stressed Latin short e/o often became ie/ue (terra > tierra, porta > puerta). These inherited sound changes have exceptions and regional scope.
