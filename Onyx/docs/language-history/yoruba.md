# Yoruba: Yoruboid branching, tone and underdots
Yoruba is Volta-Niger/Yoruboid; siblings include Itsekiri and Igala, with internal subgrouping debated. An interdisciplinary model places Proto-Yoruboid in the Niger–Benue region over c.2500–300 BCE and proposes differentiation by c.250 CE. This is low-confidence historical reconstruction, not a precise glottochronological date. Yoruba is a dialect continuum.

Standard writing uses adapted Latin letters, with 19th-century missionary standards and later reviews; Yoruba Ajami also existed without a single uniform standard. Ẹ/ẹ U+1EB8/U+1EB9, Ọ/ọ U+1ECC/U+1ECD, Ṣ/ṣ U+1E62/U+1E63. Acute U+0301 marks high tone, grave U+0300 low, mid is often unmarked. e + underdot U+0323 + acute can normalize to ẹ́. No native subscripts; Unicode has no stroke-order encoding.

Yoruba has high/mid/low tones, seven oral vowels plus nasal vowels in standard analyses, ATR vowel harmony and /kp, gb/. Canonical roots often V/CV, complicated by loans/contact. Basic order is SVO; preverbal particles mark tense/aspect/mood; serial verbs and focus matter. Tone distinguishes lexical and grammatical readings.
