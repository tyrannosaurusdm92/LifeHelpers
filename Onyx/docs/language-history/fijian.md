# Fijian: Oceanic ancestry, open syllables and orthography
Proto-Oceanic is calibrated around 3,000–3,300 BP; Proto-Central Pacific is later and differentiated around Fiji. Proto-Polynesian branched eastward. Fijian itself has eastern/western varieties; Rotuman’s exact placement is debated. These dates are models, not written attestations.

19th-century missionaries developed the Latin-based standard, with Bauan influence; print and Bible translation helped stabilize it. Letter mappings include b /mb/, d /nd/, q /ŋɡ/, g /ŋ/, c /ð/. Dr represents a rhotic sound with differing phonetic analyses. Standard writing generally needs no diacritics; academic macrons may mark length. There are no native subscripts. Unicode stores letters, not strokes.

Fijian favors open CV/V syllables and commonly penultimate stress. Basic transitive clauses are often VOS/verb-initial. Comparative examples: Proto-Oceanic *p > /v/ in Bauan *patu ‘stone’ > vatu; *s > /t/ in cited Lau examples such as *pose ‘paddle’. The latter is Lau-specific.
