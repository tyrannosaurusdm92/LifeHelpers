# Evidence and dating method

## Separate the clocks

This set uses four evidence classes: (1) inscriptions and dated manuscripts, (2) reconstructed linguistic stages based on regular correspondences, (3) archaeological or Bayesian family-date calibrations, and (4) social/orthographic milestones such as printing, school standards or official Romanization. Each module labels the kind of date it reports. A dated written object proves that a form was written by then; it does not date the origin of speech. A reconstructed proto-language has no surviving direct recording.

## Root-age requirement

The chosen families have ancestor stages older than 2,500 years, but support differs. In dates marked BP, the archaeological baseline is 1950 CE rather than the date this document is read. Latin has archaic inscriptions by the seventh–sixth centuries BCE. Proto-Oceanic uses a documented archaeological calibration of c.3,000–3,300 BP. For Mandarin, Bayesian Sino-Tibetan phylogenies estimate the wider family root at 5,871 BP, 7,184 BP and 7,983 BP in separate studies; this is a model-based date, with a 2020 study reporting a wide 95% highest posterior density interval. Late Shang writing is a separate attestation, not the emergence date of Mandarin. Hebrew’s Canaanite evidence and differentiation are placed around the Late Bronze / Early Iron transition. For Yoruba, one Cambridge overview places Niger-Congo expansion near 12,000 BP, while its internal classification and reconstruction remain debated; a closer Proto-Yoruboid model gives a broad 2500–300 BCE period and daughter differentiation around 300 CE. Neither is a precise inscription date, and the local window is not consensus glottochronology. These differences are exposed rather than compressed into false precision.

## Family trees and siblings

Siblings are branches sharing a reconstructed ancestor, not necessarily mutually intelligible languages. For Mandarin, subgroup labels and whether Jin is grouped with Mandarin vary, and the root models concern Sino-Tibetan rather than a Mandarin-specific split. Fijian is internally diverse and Polynesian is a nearby Central Pacific branch. Yoruboid higher and lower classifications are still discussed, and Niger-Congo is a macrofamily with contested deep structure. Ugaritic is Northwest Semitic, but its inclusion in narrow Canaanite classifications varies. These caveats are part of the evidence, not omissions.

## Sound correspondences

Every sound-change record names its source stage, environment, outcome, example and limitation. A comparison such as Latin *f* weakening in Castilian, Proto-Oceanic *p* : Bauan Fijian *v*, a Mandarin coda-tone change, the Canaanite vowel shift, or a Yoruba loanword syllable repair has a specific period and scope. The records are not transformations to run on arbitrary words.
