# Yoruba (standard orthography; dialect continuum): Syllable structure, prosody and sentence patterns

**Selection:** African language.  
**Lineage:** Niger-Congo (classification contested at highest levels) → Volta-Niger / Benue-Congo groupings vary → Yoruboid → Edekiri / Yoruba varieties.

Traditional Yoruba syllables are commonly analyzed as V, CV, and syllabic nasal; clusters and codas are disallowed in the conservative core phonology. The contemporary lexicon is more layered: English loans and contact can introduce clusters and coda patterns, which may be repaired, retained or hypercorrected depending on lexical history and speaker practice. Tone is associated with syllables and can distinguish words or grammatical forms. Syllable-sensitive tone restoration research found that syllabic units improved automatic tone-mark restoration compared with character-only approaches, which is practical evidence that orthography and phonology must be modeled above the code-point level.

Yoruba is commonly described as SVO, with preverbal markers and serial verb constructions. Clause meaning also depends on tone, particles and information structure. The standard writing norm uses acute for high tone, grave for low, and often leaves mid unmarked; absence of a mark in printed text can reflect editorial omission rather than the word having no tone. Dialect differences and contact mean one template cannot be applied mechanically to all speech. The profile therefore distinguishes traditional syllable structure from emerging contemporary patterns, and standard orthography from an unmarked keyboard transcript.

## Compact structural record

| Feature | Summary |
|---|---|
| Syllable profile | Traditional V/CV plus syllabic nasal; tone-bearing syllables; contemporary contact has expanded some loanword strata with clusters and codas. |
| Sentence pattern | Broadly SVO with preverbal grammatical markers, serial verbs, tone and information-structure effects. |
