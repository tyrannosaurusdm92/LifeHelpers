# Yoruba (standard orthography; dialect continuum): Lineage, root age and sibling branches

**Selection:** African language.  
**Lineage:** Niger-Congo (classification contested at highest levels) → Volta-Niger / Benue-Congo groupings vary → Yoruboid → Edekiri / Yoruba varieties.

**Root-age record:** A Cambridge overview places Niger-Congo expansion near 12,000 BP; a Language Science Press overview describes Niger-Congo as a macrofamily or phylum at least 12 millennia old while emphasizing incomplete reconstruction and uneven evidence. For the closer Yoruba lineage, Ogundiran’s interdisciplinary model places Proto-Yoruboid development in a broad 2500–300 BCE period, with daughter differentiation by about 300 CE; that window runs from roughly 4,500 to 2,300 BP.

**Evidence type:** published macrofamily expansion interpretations plus lower-level comparative classification and an archaeology / oral-history informed branch model. **Confidence:** low for an exact Niger-Congo root date or full reconstruction; low to moderate for Proto-Yoruboid calendar dates; stronger for related Yoruba, Itsekiri and Igala groupings.

**Dating limit:** The roughly 12,000 BP value is a broad family-expansion estimate, not a securely reconstructed point date for Proto-Niger-Congo. Only the early part of the Proto-Yoruboid window exceeds 2,500 years. These distinct claims should not be collapsed into a precise date for Yoruba.

**Sibling branches / comparison set:** Igala; Itsekiri / Iṣẹkiri; Yoruba varieties including Ọ̀yọ́, Èkìtì, Ìjẹ̀bú and Ìjẹ̀ṣà; Other Edekiri varieties (subgroup labels vary).

Yoruba is generally placed in Yoruboid within the Volta-Niger / Benue-Congo and broader Niger-Congo groupings. The exact higher-level classification of Niger-Congo remains debated, so the tree records commonly used labels with a warning rather than presenting every node as settled. At the lower level, comparative work treats Yoruba, Itsekiri and Igala as related Yoruboid languages; internal subgrouping and the status of varieties continue to be refined. Yoruba itself is a dialect continuum, and Standard Yoruba is a selected written norm rather than a neutral label for every regional variety.

A Cambridge overview places Niger-Congo expansion near 12,000 BP; a Language Science Press volume describes the proposed macrofamily as at least 12 millennia old. These are broad family-level interpretations. The sources also stress uneven comparative documentation, unresolved internal classification and the absence of a completed Proto-Niger-Congo lexicon and grammar. This is deep family context, not a precise Proto-Niger-Congo split date. At the closer branch level, Ogundiran’s transdisciplinary history proposes a broad Pre-Archaic period from 2500 to 300 BCE in which Proto-Yoruboid developed around the Niger–Benue confluence, followed by migration and differentiation of proto-Igala, proto-Itsekiri and proto-Yoruba by around 300 CE. Cambridge’s earlier synthesis reports very broad glottochronological estimates and stresses the last separation of Igala and Yoruba, illustrating why a precise date is not secure. The coding preserves the macrofamily age estimate and local range as different evidence records, rather than treating either as an exact date for Yoruba.
