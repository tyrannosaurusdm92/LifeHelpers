# Yoruba (standard orthography; dialect continuum): Sound changes and historical correspondences

**Selection:** African language.  
**Lineage:** Niger-Congo (classification contested at highest levels) → Volta-Niger / Benue-Congo groupings vary → Yoruboid → Edekiri / Yoruba varieties.

Standard Yoruba is tonal, with high, mid and low tone in common analyses; mid is often unmarked (a macron can disambiguate it), while acute and grave accents mark high and low on vowels and syllabic nasals. It has seven oral vowels /i e ɛ a ɔ o u/ and nasal vowels in many descriptions, with underdots distinguishing open-mid vowels in the alphabet. The labial-velar consonants /kp/ and /gb/ are single phonemes, not onset clusters. Vowel harmony based on tongue-root contrasts is a major phonological property, but analyses of the inventory and dialect patterns differ. Tone carries lexical and grammatical information, so stripped or guessed tone is not a harmless display change.

A clear diachronic example is the change in syllable patterns under English contact. Traditional Yoruba phonology strongly favors V and CV syllables, with syllabic nasals; clusters and codas in borrowed forms are often resolved by vowel insertion or deletion. Contemporary Yoruba also shows stratified loanword patterns where clusters and codas can survive, and some hypercorrection. This is documented change, not simply “bad spelling.” Comparative Yoruboid work uses recurring correspondences among Yoruba, Itsekiri and Igala to reconstruct earlier forms; individual reconstructed segments must be tied to that comparative evidence. The code avoids inventing a complete Proto-Yoruboid sound law set where the literature is still developing.
