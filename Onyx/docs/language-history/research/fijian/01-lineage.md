# Fijian (standard, Bauan-influenced): Lineage, root age and sibling branches

**Selection:** Oceanic language.  
**Lineage:** Austronesian → Malayo-Polynesian → Oceanic → Central Pacific → Fijian varieties → Standard Fijian.

**Root-age record:** Proto-Oceanic is calibrated at about 3,000–3,300 years before present (BP, using the 1950 CE baseline standard in archaeology) in archaeological-language dating work; Central Pacific differentiation is later.

**Evidence type:** archaeology-informed phylogenetic calibration plus comparative reconstruction. **Confidence:** moderate for the broad calibration; lower for fine-grained branch timing.

**Dating limit:** These are modeled dates for reconstructed ancestors, not written attestations. Fiji was an important diversification area; Fijian varieties and Polynesian languages descend through later Central Pacific history.

| Root-age audit field | Value |
|---|---|
| Requested minimum | 2500 years BP |
| Qualifying ancestor evidence | 3000 BP |

**Sibling branches / comparison set:** Eastern and Western Fijian varieties; Polynesian languages through Proto-Polynesian (including Tongan, Samoan, Māori and others); Rotuman (placement and history require care; do not call it an uncontested direct sibling).

Fijian is Austronesian → Malayo-Polynesian → Oceanic → Central Pacific. At this scale, it shares inheritance with many Oceanic languages. The most relevant nearby comparison is the Central Pacific network: eastern and western Fijian varieties and the ancestors of Polynesian languages. Polynesian is a sister branch in that wider Central Pacific history, not a dialect of Fijian. Rotuman is often discussed in regional histories, but its exact position and contact history should be labeled cautiously rather than inserted as a settled direct sibling. Modern ‘Fijian’ also covers substantial internal diversity; the standard written variety is strongly Bauan-influenced.

The Proto-Oceanic ancestor is reconstructed by comparing recurring sound correspondences across Oceanic languages. It is not an attested written language. Archaeological evidence for settlement in previously uninhabited Oceania has been used to calibrate the ancestor at roughly 3,000–3,300 years before present. Because migration across ocean barriers offers unusually strong chronological evidence, this is a more explicit calibration than many continental family dates, but it remains a model with assumptions. The profile records Proto-Oceanic and Proto-Central Pacific separately and does not treat modern national borders or standard-language labels as ancient family branches.
