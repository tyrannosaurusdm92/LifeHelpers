# Fijian (standard, Bauan-influenced): Sound changes and historical correspondences

**Selection:** Oceanic language.  
**Lineage:** Austronesian → Malayo-Polynesian → Oceanic → Central Pacific → Fijian varieties → Standard Fijian.

Proto-Oceanic phonology is reconstructed from regular sound correspondences among descendants, and its notation is conventional rather than a recording of one ancient speaker. A familiar comparison is Proto-Oceanic *patu ‘stone’ with Bauan Fijian vatu. This supports a correspondence from ancestral *p to Fijian v in that inherited set; it is not a productive rule for every Fijian v or every modern loan. Other Oceanic languages show different reflexes, such as loss of *p between vowels in Takia, demonstrating why comparisons must name the branch and environment.

Modern standard Fijian has five vowel qualities and a set of consonant spellings whose values differ from English expectations: b commonly represents a prenasalized voiced stop [mb], d [nd], q [ŋg], g [ŋ] and c [ð]; dr is a special sequence whose phonetic analysis and dialect realization require care. Comparative work reconstructs mergers and later phonemic developments across Proto-Malayo-Polynesian and Proto-Oceanic; the word-initial prenasalized series arose historically from nasal-plus-obstruent sequences. Long vowel sequences, loans and dialect contact complicate a simple one-to-one history. The files store attested correspondences with their branch and scope, not universal sound substitutions.
