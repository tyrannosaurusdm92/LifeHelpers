## Syllables, prosody, and syntax

Egyptian syntax changes substantially through its stages: word order, verbal constructions, and clause-linking strategies develop over millennia. Old and Middle Egyptian can show verb-initial patterns in particular constructions, while later Egyptian and Coptic restructure verbal and analytic constructions. Syllable structure is reconstructed cautiously from consonantal scripts and later Coptic evidence; do not treat a transliteration as phonetic syllabification.

The descriptive labels here are broad summaries, not templates for every dialect or genre. Compare attested forms by date and register; syntax may change while a writing convention remains conservative. Phonological syllables, script units, aksharas, and Unicode grapheme clusters are distinct analytical units.
