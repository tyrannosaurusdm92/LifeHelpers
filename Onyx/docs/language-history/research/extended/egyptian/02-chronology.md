## Chronology and evidence

Hieroglyphic writing appears in Egypt by the end of the fourth millennium BCE, with fully developed textual evidence by the early dynastic/Old Kingdom periods. The language stages extend from Old Egyptian through Coptic, which remained spoken into the medieval period and survives liturgically. Dating a script, a text, and a reconstructed ancestor are distinct tasks.

**Dating statement:** Published Proto-Afroasiatic chronology proposals vary widely: some place it around 8,000–10,000 years ago and others at 15,000 years or more. The 8,000 BP value is the younger edge of the cited proposals, not a consensus estimate; Egyptian hieroglyphic attestation is a separate datum.

Keep three clocks separate: (1) reconstructed ancestor age, (2) earliest surviving written attestation, and (3) emergence of a standardized modern variety. They answer different questions. A lack of early writing is not evidence that a language did not exist.
