## Family placement

Ancient Egyptian is a distinct branch of Afroasiatic, not a sister dialect of Arabic or Hebrew. Its recorded stages are conventionally Old Egyptian, Middle Egyptian, Late Egyptian, Demotic, and Coptic, each reflecting chronological and register differences. Comparative Afroasiatic ties link Egyptian with Semitic, Berber, Cushitic, Chadic, and other groupings, but deep subgroup relationships and the proto-language date are debated.

**Proposed root:** Proto-Afroasiatic (deep reconstructed ancestor; Egyptian is one daughter branch) — 8000–15000 BP, using 1950 CE as the conventional BP origin. The qualifying point estimate is 8000 BP. Confidence: very low for exact deep dates and homeland; proposed dates differ by millennia. This date concerns the stated ancestor; it is not an age claim for the modern language. 

**Siblings and comparisons:**
- Semitic languages (distinct Afroasiatic branch; includes Aramaic, Hebrew, Arabic)
- Berber/Amazigh languages (distinct Afroasiatic branch)
- Cushitic languages (distinct Afroasiatic branch)
- Chadic languages (distinct Afroasiatic branch)
- Omotic languages (classification debated)

Do not flatten distant relatives and immediate sister groups into a single branching level.
