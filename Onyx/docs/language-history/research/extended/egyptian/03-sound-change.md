## Sound history

Egyptian writing often omits vowels, so the phonology of many stages is reconstructed with uncertainty. Consonantal changes, weakening/loss of final consonants, and changes in verbal morphology help distinguish stages; Coptic, written with an alphabetic Greek-derived script supplemented by Egyptian signs, makes vowels more visible. Conventional Egyptological transliteration is not itself a direct recording of ancient pronunciation.

For reliable sound-change claims, identify the source stage, daughter branch, conditioning environment, and counterexamples. Orthographic similarity alone cannot establish cognacy or sound correspondence. Reconstructed segments use an asterisk and remain hypotheses.
