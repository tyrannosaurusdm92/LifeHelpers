## Script, Unicode, diacritics, and glyphs

Three Georgian scripts are historically recognized: Asomtavruli/Mrgvlovani, Nuskhuri, and Mkhedruli. Asomtavruli is the monumental/majuscule form; Nuskhuri is the more compact ecclesiastical minuscule, used with Asomtavruli in Khutsuri; Mkhedruli became the everyday civil hand and predominates today. Modern Mtavruli uppercase forms have separate Unicode encoding. Fifth-century inscriptions provide secure early evidence; claims of a pre-Christian alphabet remain disputed. Georgian scripts are historical systems, not merely font styles.

**Unicode model:** Georgian script encoding across Georgian U+10A0–U+10FF, Georgian Supplement U+2D00–U+2D2F, and Georgian Extended U+1C90–U+1CBF. Unicode encodes characters, not historical pen strokes or mandatory letterforms.

| Sample | Code points | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ა | U+10D0 | Mkhedruli a |
| Ⴀ | U+10A0 | Asomtavruli an |
| ⴀ | U+2D00 | Nuskhuri an |
| Ა | U+1C90 | Mtavruli an |

**Subscripts:** Georgian alphabetic spelling has no productive subscript-letter system.

**Diacritics and combining marks:** Modern standard spelling usually does not mark stress or tone with diacritics. Scholarly transliteration may use macrons, dots, or apostrophes; those belong to a declared transcription convention, not to ordinary Georgian Unicode spelling.

**Glyph strokes and layout:** Unicode scalar values identify characters; stroke order and regional glyph design are paleographic/font matters. Mkhedruli and Mtavruli have corresponding code points where encoded, while shaping and font support affect appearance.

Unicode encodes characters, not the complete historical calligraphic or palaeographic process. Record source form, normalization, transliteration, and rendering assumptions separately.
