## Sound history

Kartvelian comparison includes rich consonant contrasts, ejectives, and complex consonant sequences. Georgian has stop series conventionally described by voicing and aspiration/ejective contrasts; exact phonetic descriptions vary by analysis and dialect. Sound correspondences must be stated as sets, not as universal letter substitutions. Vowel reduction, dialect contact, and historical borrowing complicate simple sound laws.

For reliable sound-change claims, identify the source stage, daughter branch, conditioning environment, and counterexamples. Orthographic similarity alone cannot establish cognacy or sound correspondence. Reconstructed segments use an asterisk and remain hypotheses.
