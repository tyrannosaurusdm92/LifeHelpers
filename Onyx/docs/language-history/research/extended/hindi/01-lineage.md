## Family placement

Hindi is New Indo-Aryan and belongs to the Indo-Iranian branch of Indo-European. Its history is not a direct, uninterrupted “Sanskrit became Hindi” line: Middle Indo-Aryan varieties (including Prakrits and Apabhraṃśa traditions) intervene, and reconstruction depends on comparative evidence. Hindustani describes a shared colloquial base from which standardized Hindi and Urdu developed distinct registers and scripts.

**Proposed root:** Proto-Indo-Aryan (with deeper Proto-Indo-Iranian comparison) — 3200–3800 BP, using 1950 CE as the conventional BP origin. The qualifying point estimate is 3500 BP. Confidence: moderate for Vedic attestation horizon; lower for exact proto-stage dating. This date concerns the stated ancestor; it is not an age claim for the modern language. 

**Siblings and comparisons:**
- Urdu (closely related standard register in the Hindustani continuum; Perso-Arabic script)
- Punjabi (Northwestern Indo-Aryan)
- Gujarati (Western Indo-Aryan)
- Bengali (Eastern Indo-Aryan)
- Marathi (Southern Indo-Aryan)

Do not flatten distant relatives and immediate sister groups into a single branching level.
