## Syllables, prosody, and syntax

Aramaic clause order varies by period, genre, and discourse; verb-subject-object and subject-verb-object patterns are both represented in descriptions. Semitic syllables typically center on a vowel nucleus and allow consonant clusters/codas, with dialect-specific restrictions. Tense-aspect systems and periphrastic constructions shift across the long record; an Imperial text cannot stand for all modern dialects.

The descriptive labels here are broad summaries, not templates for every dialect or genre. Compare attested forms by date and register; syntax may change while a writing convention remains conservative. Phonological syllables, script units, aksharas, and Unicode grapheme clusters are distinct analytical units.
