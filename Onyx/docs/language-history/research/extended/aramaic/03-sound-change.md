## Sound history

Semitic root-and-pattern morphology organizes much Aramaic vocabulary, alongside affixal morphology. Historical varieties show changes in emphatic consonants, gutturals, vowels, and consonant clusters; outcomes vary by branch and period. Transliteration conventions differ in representing emphatics, spirantization, vowel length, and matres lectionis, so a sound history must specify dialect and script tradition.

For reliable sound-change claims, identify the source stage, daughter branch, conditioning environment, and counterexamples. Orthographic similarity alone cannot establish cognacy or sound correspondence. Reconstructed segments use an asterisk and remain hypotheses.
