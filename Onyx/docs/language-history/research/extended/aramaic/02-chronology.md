## Chronology and evidence

Aramaic inscriptions appear in the early first millennium BCE, with dating and interpretation of the earliest examples actively discussed. Imperial/Official Aramaic spread widely through Neo-Assyrian, Babylonian, and Achaemenid administrative networks, while local spoken varieties continued to diversify. Later branches include Syriac, Jewish Aramaic traditions, Mandaic, and modern Neo-Aramaic varieties; their written histories differ.

**Dating statement:** Proto-Northwest Semitic is reconstructed from shared correspondences and branches including Aramaic and Canaanite; Old Aramaic inscriptions by the tenth–ninth centuries BCE provide a documentary floor. The 2,850 BP value dates the attested branch benchmark, not the exact proto-language split.

Keep three clocks separate: (1) reconstructed ancestor age, (2) earliest surviving written attestation, and (3) emergence of a standardized modern variety. They answer different questions. A lack of early writing is not evidence that a language did not exist.
