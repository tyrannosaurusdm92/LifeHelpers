## Family placement

Aramaic is Northwest Semitic within Semitic and Afroasiatic. It is historically diverse: Old, Imperial/Official, Middle, and later Eastern and Western varieties are labels for phases and traditions, not one homogeneous language at all dates. Its close comparisons include Hebrew and Phoenician, but Aramaic is not a form of Hebrew. Many Jewish, Christian, and regional literary traditions used distinct Aramaic dialects.

**Proposed root:** Proto-Northwest Semitic → Aramaic branch (Old Aramaic attested) — 2500–3500 BP, using 1950 CE as the conventional BP origin. The qualifying point estimate is 2850 BP. Confidence: high for early first-millennium BCE written evidence; branch boundaries and exact first dates are debated. This date concerns the stated ancestor; it is not an age claim for the modern language. 

**Siblings and comparisons:**
- Hebrew (Northwest Semitic; Canaanite)
- Phoenician (Northwest Semitic; Canaanite)
- Ugaritic (Northwest Semitic; extinct, distinct branch placement debated)
- Arabic (Central Semitic, not a dialect of Aramaic)
- Modern Aramaic varieties (Neo-Aramaic branches; internal classifications vary)

Do not flatten distant relatives and immediate sister groups into a single branching level.
