## Chronology and evidence

Linear B is attested in administrative archives from the Late Bronze Age, roughly the fifteenth through twelfth centuries BCE, with chronology depending on site and tablet context. Tablets were often short-lived palace records and baked during destruction events; they are not representative literary corpora. The 1952 decipherment by Michael Ventris, with John Chadwick’s linguistic collaboration, established the language as Greek.

**Dating statement:** Linear B tablets from the second millennium BCE directly record Mycenaean Greek; comparative Greek dialect history reconstructs earlier Proto-Greek ancestry.

Keep three clocks separate: (1) reconstructed ancestor age, (2) earliest surviving written attestation, and (3) emergence of a standardized modern variety. They answer different questions. A lack of early writing is not evidence that a language did not exist.
