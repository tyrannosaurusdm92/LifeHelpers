## Syllables, prosody, and syntax

Mycenaean Greek has inflectional morphology inherited into later Greek, but tablet genre constrains syntax: inventories and ration lists favor compressed administrative formulae. The syllabary records syllabic chunks, not syllables transparently in the phonological sense, because codas and clusters are underrepresented. Stress and vowel quantity are not systematically marked by Linear B signs.

The descriptive labels here are broad summaries, not templates for every dialect or genre. Compare attested forms by date and register; syntax may change while a writing convention remains conservative. Phonological syllables, script units, aksharas, and Unicode grapheme clusters are distinct analytical units.
