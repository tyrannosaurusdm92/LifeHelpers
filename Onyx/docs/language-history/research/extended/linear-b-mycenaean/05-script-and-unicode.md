## Script, Unicode, diacritics, and glyphs

Linear B descends from/adapts the earlier Linear A sign system for Greek. It combines syllabograms with ideograms and numerical signs, written primarily left-to-right on clay tablets. The script was deciphered, but some sign values and readings remain disputed or unknown. Editorial transliterations use hyphens between signs; a notation like pa₂ is a Latin scholarly label with a subscript numeral, not a special Linear B glyph.

**Unicode model:** Linear B Syllabary U+10000–U+1007F; Linear B Ideograms U+10080–U+100FF; Aegean Numbers U+10100–U+1013F. Unicode characters represent encoded signs; editorial transliteration systems are separate.

| Sample | Code points | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 𐀀 | U+10000 | Linear B Syllable B008 A |
| 𐀁 | U+10001 | Linear B Syllable B038 E |
| 𐀒 | U+10012 | Linear B Syllable B001 DA |
| 𐀀𐀀 | U+10000, U+10000 | Two encoded signs; no implicit hyphen or word boundary |

**Subscripts:** Subscript numerals in transliterations (e.g. pa₂) disambiguate sign labels or readings; they are Latin editorial characters, typically U+2082, not native subscripts in Linear B.

**Diacritics and combining marks:** Linear B does not mark Greek accentuation or vowel length in a way comparable to later Greek orthography. Diacritics used in scholarly Greek forms belong to transliteration/editorial practice. Preserve original sign sequence separately from normalized transliteration.

**Glyph strokes and layout:** Paleographic variation among scribes is essential evidence. Unicode encodes sign identity but does not specify wedge/stroke order, tablet orientation, line layout, or archaeological damage.

Unicode encodes characters, not the complete historical calligraphic or palaeographic process. Record source form, normalization, transliteration, and rendering assumptions separately.
