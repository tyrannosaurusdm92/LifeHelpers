# Mandarin Chinese (Standard Mandarin focus): Sound changes and historical correspondences

**Selection:** Asian / Sinitic language.  
**Lineage:** Sino-Tibetan → Sinitic → Mandarin → Standard Mandarin.

Old Chinese phonology is reconstructed, not read directly off characters. Evidence comes from rhymes, shared phonetic components, Middle Chinese rhyme dictionaries and later dialects, with alternative reconstructions in the literature. Reconstructions generally posit contrasts and syllable shapes unlike modern Mandarin. Middle Chinese retained stop codas /-p, -t, -k/ in the entering-tone category; these codas were lost in Mandarin varieties, and the lexical items were distributed among later tone categories. The exact outcomes vary by initial class and Mandarin history. Across Sinitic branches, earlier onset voicing conditioned tone/register splits, but details differ by branch.

Modern Standard Mandarin has four lexical tones and a neutral tone, restricted syllable structures, and mainly nasal finals /n, ŋ/; historical stop codas are absent. Tone sandhi such as third-tone alternation is a synchronic phonological process and must not be confused with the historical origin of the tone system. Romanized Pinyin marks tones for learning and transcription, while Han characters are not alphabetic spellings of one segment at a time. The module records evidence type and period for each correspondence and does not pretend that modern pronunciations can be obtained by applying three simple sound laws to oracle-bone writing.
