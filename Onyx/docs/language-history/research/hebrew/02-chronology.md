# Hebrew (Biblical through Modern): Chronology and evidence

**Selection:** Middle Eastern language.  
**Lineage:** Afro-Asiatic → Semitic → West Semitic → Central Semitic → Northwest Semitic → Canaanite → Hebrew.

Canaanite writing and speech are visible in Late Bronze evidence, including material where Canaanite forms are represented through other scribal languages and scripts. Hebrew becomes clearly distinguishable in Iron Age evidence. The language’s literary and inscriptional record then develops across ancient Israel and Judah, the exile and Second Temple periods, rabbinic Hebrew, medieval textual traditions and modern revival. These are overlapping stages rather than clean replacements. The Hebrew Bible also preserves poetry and prose from different periods, and later vocalization systems document reading traditions rather than original contemporaneous vowel spellings.

Script chronology differs from language chronology. Hebrew first used a Phoenician-derived Paleo-Hebrew alphabet. During the Persian period, an Aramaic-derived square script increasingly displaced it, though Paleo-Hebrew persisted for special uses into the Second Temple era. Masoretic communities later added niqqud and cantillation signs to preserve reading and recitation. Everyday modern Hebrew is usually written without these marks. The code labels each date as linguistic, epigraphic, scribal or revival evidence so a change of script is not mistaken for a language family split.
