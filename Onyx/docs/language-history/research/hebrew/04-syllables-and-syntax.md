# Hebrew (Biblical through Modern): Syllable structure, prosody and sentence patterns

**Selection:** Middle Eastern language.  
**Lineage:** Afro-Asiatic → Semitic → West Semitic → Central Semitic → Northwest Semitic → Canaanite → Hebrew.

Modern Hebrew syllables can be broadly represented as (C)(C)V(C), subject to restrictions on onset clusters and codas. Earlier Hebrew stages had their own vowel-length, reduction and gemination patterns, and the modern inventory is smaller in many pronunciations. Niqqud makes vowel information visible in pointed texts, while unpointed everyday spelling relies on lexical knowledge and context. Stress is often ultimate or penultimate, but morphology, loanwords and historical stages need separate treatment. The template is descriptive shorthand, not a complete syllabifier.

Biblical Hebrew verbal clauses often place the verb before the subject, but word order is affected by discourse, clause type and narrative style. Modern Hebrew is commonly SVO, while allowing fronting and other constructions. The language retains Semitic root-pattern morphology, binyan verbal templates, agreement and the construct state. Syntax has changed over time and has interacted with Aramaic, later languages and modern revival. A historical profile must therefore name the period: a Biblical clause, a Masoretic pointing tradition and a contemporary Israeli utterance do not instantiate one static grammar.

## Compact structural record

| Feature | Summary |
|---|---|
| Syllable profile | Modern broad pattern (C)(C)V(C), with cluster restrictions; earlier stages had distinct vowel, length and gemination systems. |
| Sentence pattern | Biblical verbal clauses often VSO; Modern Hebrew commonly SVO. Root-pattern morphology and construct state are central. |
