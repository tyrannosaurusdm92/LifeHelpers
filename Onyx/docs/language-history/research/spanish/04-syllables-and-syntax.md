# Spanish: Syllable structure, prosody and sentence patterns

**Selection:** Romance language.  
**Lineage:** Indo-European → Italic → Latin → Romance → Ibero-Romance → Castilian / Spanish.

A practical broad Spanish syllable template is (C)(C)V(C), with onset and coda restrictions shaped by sonority, dialect, word history and loan adaptation. Spanish has five oral vowel phonemes. Vowel sequences may form diphthongs or triphthongs, or hiatus; written accent rules do not simply mirror every speaker’s phonetic syllabification. Default lexical stress is predictable from the final letter, and an acute accent marks stress when spelling rules require it, as well as some grammatical distinctions. The RAE’s orthographic rules treat specified vowel sequences consistently for accentuation even where phonetic realization may vary.

Unmarked declaratives commonly follow subject–verb–object order, but Spanish permits flexible order for discourse focus and often omits a recoverable subject pronoun. Verb agreement, clitics, prepositions and information structure interact with word order. A sentence diagram for Spanish therefore needs to record both a neutral order and pragmatic alternatives; it should not infer a single fixed order from a short example. The profile lists broad patterns and directs detailed parsing to a grammar rather than pretending that a compact syllable expression or one SVO label captures every construction.

| Feature | Summary |
