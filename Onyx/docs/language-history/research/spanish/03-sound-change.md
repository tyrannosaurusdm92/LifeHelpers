# Spanish: Sound changes and historical correspondences

**Selection:** Romance language.  
**Lineage:** Indo-European → Italic → Latin → Romance → Ibero-Romance → Castilian / Spanish.

Several characteristic Castilian developments can be compared with Latin while remaining explicitly conditioned. Stressed Latin short-mid vowels often diphthongized: compare terra with tierra and porta with puerta. This pattern is associated with stressed syllables and has lexical and historical exceptions; it is not a rule that converts every written e or o. In parts of northern Iberian Romance, Latin word-initial /f/ before a vowel passed through an aspirated stage commonly written h and later lost its aspiration in much of standard Spanish: farina → medieval harina → modern silent h, and filium → hijo. /f/ remains in many consonant clusters, learned words and later borrowings, so the spelling h is not a live sound rule.

Latin voiceless stops between vowels often became voiced in inherited Spanish words, as in vita → vida; in contemporary Spanish the voiced stop phonemes commonly have approximant realizations in these positions. Latin case inflection also weakened across the transition to Romance, with prepositions, clitics and constituent order carrying more grammatical work. These are broad historical tendencies, not a universal derivation engine. Dialect, chronology, analogy and lexical history explain exceptions. The JavaScript rules are evidence records with scope notes, not code that rewrites arbitrary Latin words.
