# Spanish: Chronology and evidence

**Selection:** Romance language.  
**Lineage:** Indo-European → Italic → Latin → Romance → Ibero-Romance → Castilian / Spanish.

The chronology distinguishes dated writing from reconstructed speech. Early Latin inscriptions from the seventh–sixth centuries BCE are the firmest anchor; the exact age and interpretation of individual objects can be debated. Latin later gains more epigraphic and literary documentation. During the first millennium CE, regional spoken Latin changes and differentiates; there is no single day when Latin ‘became’ Spanish. Medieval Iberian records show early Romance varieties and Castilian written practice. The 1492 grammar by Antonio de Nebrija is a milestone in codification, while printing, administration, colonial expansion and later academy work spread and standardize particular forms. None of these is a family split date.

Spanish spelling records historical layers unevenly. Some inherited sound changes are reflected in spelling, others were later regularized, and learned words often retain forms closer to Latin than inherited everyday words. The code therefore stores chronology rows with an evidence label: inscription, literary record, orthographic milestone, or reconstruction. A reconstructed stage should never be presented as an artifact date, and a modern standard should not be projected unchanged into medieval speech.
