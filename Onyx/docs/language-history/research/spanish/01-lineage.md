# Spanish: Lineage, root age and sibling branches

**Selection:** Romance language.  
**Lineage:** Indo-European → Italic → Latin → Romance → Ibero-Romance → Castilian / Spanish.

**Root-age record:** Latin inscriptions survive from the seventh–sixth centuries BCE; some individual early inscriptions have contested dating or language attribution.

**Evidence type:** inscriptional attestation. **Confidence:** high for attestation; moderate for individual object dating.

**Dating limit:** This is the age of an ancestor and written record, not the birth date of Spanish. Spoken Latin diversified regionally over centuries; Spanish is a medieval Romance language.

**Sibling branches / comparison set:** Portuguese and Galician; Astur-Leonese; Aragonese; Catalan (its position within Romance classifications is described differently); French; Italian; Sardinian; Romanian.

Spanish belongs to Romance, the descendant group of Latin, within Indo-European. A useful working path is Indo-European → Italic → Latin → Romance → Ibero-Romance → Castilian/Spanish. These labels describe different levels: Latin is an attested language; Proto-Italic and Proto-Indo-European are reconstructed ancestors; Romance is a family of daughter languages, not a single uniform stage. Roman Latin is documented in archaic inscriptions by the seventh–sixth centuries BCE, while the familiar medieval and modern forms of Spanish arose much later. The Roman expansion spread Latin across regions whose speech later developed into multiple Romance languages.

Spanish has close comparisons with Portuguese and Galician and broader Romance comparisons with Astur-Leonese, Aragonese, Catalan, French, Italian, Sardinian and Romanian. The exact grouping and status of some varieties varies among classification traditions. Shared descent is established through recurring sound correspondences and inherited morphology, not simply shared vocabulary or present-day mutual understanding. Contact, learned Latin vocabulary and later standardization complicate every branch boundary. This profile treats the tree as a useful model and records where classification is not uniform.
