# Comparative language histories: 12 profiles

Profiles: [Spanish](spanish.md), [Fijian](fijian.md), [Mandarin](mandarin.md), [Yoruba](yoruba.md), [Hebrew](hebrew.md), [Georgian](georgian.md), [Khmer](khmer.md), [Hindi](hindi.md), [Aramaic](aramaic.md), [Proto-Oceanic](proto-oceanic.md), [Ancient Egyptian](egyptian.md), [Mycenaean Greek recorded in Linear B](linear-b-mycenaean.md). Research navigation: [five original profiles](research/README.md) and [seven additions](research/extended/README.md). The original stable code API remains at `Onyx/js/language-history/research/index.js`; additions are in `extended-index.js`.

| Choice | Historical root or early stage | Sibling comparison | Dating caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spanish | Archaic Latin inscriptions, seventh–sixth c. BCE | Romance descendants including Portuguese/Galician and broader Romance relatives | Spanish formed later; inscriptions date Latin writing, not modern Spanish |
| Fijian | Proto-Oceanic c. 3,000–3,300 BP (1950 baseline) calibration | Fijian varieties and Polynesian through later Central Pacific history | Archaeology-informed model, not direct language attestation |
| Mandarin | Sino-Tibetan root estimates c. 5,871–7,983 BP; Old Chinese written evidence in Late Shang | Sinitic branches including Wu, Yue, Min, Hakka, Gan, Xiang, Jin and others; wider Tibeto-Burman comparisons | Modeled family dates vary; neither Shang writing nor the family root dates Mandarin directly |
| Yoruba | Niger-Congo expansion estimate near 12,000 BP; local Proto-Yoruboid model 2500–300 BCE | Itsekiri, Igala and Yoruba varieties | Macrofamily expansion is broad and classification-sensitive; local range is model-specific |
| Hebrew | Canaanite evidence c. 1550–1200 BCE; Hebrew differentiates gradually near Late Bronze / Early Iron | Phoenician, Ammonite, Moabite, Edomite; Aramaic close but distinct | Gradual boundary, not a single founding date |
| Georgian | Proto-Kartvelian; historical models diverge from the fourth–third millennia BCE to >12,500 BP | Svan, Mingrelian, Laz | The 5,000 BP audit value is the younger disputed scenario, not consensus |
| Khmer | Proto-Austroasiatic modeled at 3,000–4,500 BP | Mon, Vietnamese, Khasi and other Austroasiatic branches | Macrofamily chronology and subgrouping remain debated |
| Hindi | Proto-Indo-Aryan; Vedic oral texts from c. 1500 BCE | Urdu/Hindustani, Punjabi, Gujarati, Bengali, Marathi | Not a direct Sanskrit-to-Hindi line |
| Aramaic | Northwest Semitic; Old Aramaic inscriptions by 10th–9th c. BCE | Hebrew, Phoenician, Ugaritic; later Aramaic branches | Earliest inscription dates are debated |
| Proto-Oceanic | Reconstructed at c. 3,000–3,300 BP | Central Pacific, Micronesian, Admiralty and western Oceanic branches | Comparative reconstruction, no native written record |
| Ancient Egyptian | Proto-Afroasiatic estimates vary roughly 8,000–15,000+ BP | Semitic, Berber, Cushitic, Chadic, Omotic | Root date estimates are deeply contested; Egyptian records attest a later daughter branch |
| Mycenaean Greek / Linear B | Proto-Greek ancestor; Mycenaean written c. 1450–1150 BCE | Arcado-Cypriot, Attic-Ionic, Aeolic, Doric | Linear B is the script, Mycenaean Greek the language |

Age refers to an ancestor or an attested historical stage, not an unchanged modern language. Reconstructed stages are hypotheses. Inscriptions, archaeology-calibrated phylogenies and manuscript dates are different kinds of evidence. Unicode encodes characters and marks, not pen strokes or stroke order.
