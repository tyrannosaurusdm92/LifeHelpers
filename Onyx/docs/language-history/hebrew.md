# Hebrew: Canaanite inheritance and script succession
Canaanite is attested c.1550–1200 BCE; Hebrew differentiated around the Late Bronze/Iron transition, roughly 3,200 years ago. Phoenician, Ammonite, Moabite and Edomite are close siblings. Ugaritic is related Northwest Semitic but normally outside narrower Canaanite. The boundary is gradual.

Early Hebrew used Phoenician-derived Paleo-Hebrew. An Aramaic-derived square script became dominant in the Persian period; Paleo-Hebrew persisted in some contexts. Masoretic readers added niqqud vowel points and cantillation marks; modern everyday text usually omits them. Hebrew is RTL with 22 consonants. Block U+0590–U+05FF; final forms ך U+05DA, ם U+05DD, ן U+05DF, ף U+05E3, ץ U+05E5. Niqqud are combining marks (e.g. sheva U+05B0, qamats U+05B8), not subscripts. Unicode does not encode physical strokes.

Modern syllables often (C)(C)V(C). Many Israeli speakers merge historical emphatics/pharyngeals; /ħ/ may merge with /x/, /ʔ/ may be lost, and gemination is commonly absent. Biblical clauses often verb-initial; Modern Hebrew commonly SVO with fronting. Root-pattern binyanim and construct state are central.
