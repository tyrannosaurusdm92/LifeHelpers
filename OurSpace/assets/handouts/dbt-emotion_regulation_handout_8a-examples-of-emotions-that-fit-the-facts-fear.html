Emotion Regulation Handout 8a (Emotion Regulation Worksheet 5) Examples of Emotions That Fit the Facts Fear 1. There is a threat to your life or that of someone you care about. 2. There is a threat to your health or that of someone you care about. 3. There is a threat to your well-being or that of someone you care about. 4. Other: Anger 1. An important goal is blocked or a desired activity is interrupted or prevented. 2. You or someone you care about is attacked or hurt by others. 3. You or someone you care about is insulted or threatened by others. 4. The integrity or status of your social group is offended or threatened. 5. Other: Disgust 1. Something you are in contact with could poison or contaminate you. 2. Somebody whom you deeply dislike is touching you or someone you care about. 3. You are around a person or group whose behavior or thinking could seriously damage or harmfully influence you or the group you are part of. 4. Other: Envy 1. Another person or group gets or has things you don’t have that you want or need. 2. Other: Jealousy 1. A very important and desired relationship or object in your life is in danger of being damaged or lost. 2. Someone is threatening to take a valued relationship or object away from you. 3. Other: Love 1. Loving a person, animal, or object enhances quality of life for you or for those you care about. 2. Loving a person, animal, or object increases your chances of attaining your own personal goals. 3. Other: Sadness 1. You have lost something or someone permanently. 2. Things are not the way you wanted or expected and hoped them to be. 3. Other: Shame 1. You will be rejected by a person or group you care about if characteristics of yourself or of your behavior are made public. 2. Other: Guilt 1. Your own behavior violates your own values or moral code. 2. Other: Intensity and duration of an emotion are justified by: 1. How likely it is that the expected outcomes will occur. 2. How great and/or important the outcomes are. 3. How effective the emotion is in your life now.

OurSpace shared-use support notes

Emotion work starts with the body and access needs. Hunger, pain, oxygen, sleep attacks, MCAS reactions, trauma cues, and exhaustion count.

Tiny steps

  • Name the emotion.
  • Check body basics: breathing, pain, food/water, meds, temperature, sensory load.
  • Lower the demand by one size.
  • Choose soothe, solve, pause, or ask for help.

Flare-day versions

  • Minimum: One breath, one label, one safe message, or one checkbox.
  • Medium: Two to five minutes with everything reachable from bed/chair.
  • Full: Use the original worksheet/handout when both bodies and brains have capacity.

Accessibility modifications

  • Use low-exertion regulation first.
  • Allow anger and grief without turning them into shame.
  • Track patterns without moral judgment.

Relationship and caregiver notes

  • William's needs and Jasper's capacity both count.
  • Ask before helping; ask before escalating; name the smallest next step.
  • Pause means pause, not abandonment. I am coming back. I care about us. I need a smaller next step.
  • What is needed, what capacity exists, what can be smaller, and what can wait?

Avoid or adjust

  • Do not demand immediate calm during respiratory distress, pain flares, dissociation, or overload.
