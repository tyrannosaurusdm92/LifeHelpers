Distress Tolerance Handout 19 (Distress Tolerance Worksheet 16) Community Reinforcement Community reinforcement means replacing addiction reinforcers with abstinence reinforcers. Reinforcing Abstinence Is Critical Reinforcers in your environment play a powerful role in encouraging or discouraging addictive behaviors. To stop addictive behavior, you have to figure out how to make a lifestyle without your addictive behaviors more rewarding than a lifestyle with your addictive behaviors. You have to find a way to get behaviors incompatible with addictions to pay off and be rewarded by those around you. Willpower is not sufficient. If it were, we would all be perfect! Replace Addiction Reinforcers with Abstinence Reinforcers Begin a series of action steps that will increase your chances of accumulating positive events to replace addictive behavior.   Search for people to spend time with who aren’t addicted.   Increase the number of enjoyable activities you engage in that do not involve your addiction.   If you cannot decide what people or activities you like, sample a lot of different groups of people and a lot of different activities. Abstinence Sampling   Commit to days off your addiction, and observe the benefits that naturally occur.   Temporarily avoid high-risk addiction triggers, and replace these with competing behaviors to get you through the sampling period.   Observe all the extra positive events occurring when you are not engaging in addictive behaviors. OurSpace shared-use support notes

Distress tolerance means getting through the wave without adding harm. Use gentle, medically safer versions first.

Tiny steps

  • Lower danger first.
  • Reduce stimulation.
  • Send a one-line safety/capacity message.
  • Choose one body-safe skill for 30 seconds.
  • Reassess before doing more.

Flare-day versions

  • Minimum: One breath, one label, one safe message, or one checkbox.
  • Medium: Two to five minutes with everything reachable from bed/chair.
  • Full: Use the original worksheet/handout when both bodies and brains have capacity.

Accessibility modifications

  • Replace intense exercise with hand squeeze, paced exhale, safe fidget, weighted comfort if tolerated, or visual grounding.
  • Avoid cold shock/breath-holding/intense exercise unless already clinician-approved and safe that day.
  • Keep oxygen, rescue meds, allergy plan, mobility aids, and exit routes accessible.

Relationship and caregiver notes

  • William's needs and Jasper's capacity both count.
  • Ask before helping; ask before escalating; name the smallest next step.
  • Pause means pause, not abandonment. I am coming back. I care about us. I need a smaller next step.
  • What is needed, what capacity exists, what can be smaller, and what can wait?

Avoid or adjust

  • Do not use crisis skills as punishment, sensory flooding, or exposure without consent.
