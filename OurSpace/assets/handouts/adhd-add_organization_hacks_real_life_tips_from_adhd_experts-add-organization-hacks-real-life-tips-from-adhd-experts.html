Here's what they said! 2 of 9 Michael Laskoff, CEO of abito.com 1. Buy bright and shiny objects. When it comes to personal effects — keys, wallet, pens, notebooks, umbrella — I avoid black; it's easy to overlook and lose. 2. Know what you will forget. I forget a meeting time almost as soon as someone tells me. Knowing that, I make an appointment only when I can write it down on my calendar. 3. Dress the night before. I’m not a morning person, so I lay out my clothes and critical items for the next day the previous evening. 4. Downsize your desk. Give me a flat surface, and I will pile paper on it. And since I can’t stop piling, I opted for a small desk. It limits my potential to create paper-based chaos. 5. Be redundant. I use multiple “alarms” — setting a clock, programming a cell phone, asking people to call me — to remind me of things. OurSpace shared-use support notes

Cleaning is access care, not a worth test. Use scent-safe, seated, tiny-zone cleaning and stop before flare, resentment, or breathing risk.

Tiny steps

  • Pick one surface or one item type.
  • Put supplies within reach.
  • Work for 30 seconds to 5 minutes.
  • Bag, basket, wipe, or relocate one thing.
  • Stop and record the next tiny step.

Flare-day versions

  • Minimum: One breath, one label, one safe message, or one checkbox.
  • Medium: Two to five minutes with everything reachable from bed/chair.
  • Full: Use the original worksheet/handout when both bodies and brains have capacity.

Accessibility modifications

  • Default to fragrance-free/MCAS-aware supplies.
  • No scrubbing marathons, bending marathons, or heavy lifting.
  • Use chair/bed-level sorting, grabbers, rolling bins, and caregiver capacity checks.

Relationship and caregiver notes

  • William's needs and Jasper's capacity both count.
  • Ask before helping; ask before escalating; name the smallest next step.
  • Pause means pause, not abandonment. I am coming back. I care about us. I need a smaller next step.
  • What is needed, what capacity exists, what can be smaller, and what can wait?

Avoid or adjust

  • Do not frame clutter as laziness or cleanliness as morality.
