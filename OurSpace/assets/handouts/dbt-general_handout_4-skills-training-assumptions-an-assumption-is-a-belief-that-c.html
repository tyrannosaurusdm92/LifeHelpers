☐ General Handout 4 Skills Training Assumptions An assumption is a belief that cannot be proved, but we agree to abide by it anyway. 1. People are doing the best they can. All people at any given point in time are doing the best they can. 2. People want to improve. The common characteristic of all people is that they want to improve their lives and be happy. 3. People need to do better, try harder, and be more motivated to change.* The fact that people are doing the best they can, and want to do even better, does not mean that these things are enough to solve the problem. 4. People may not have caused all of our own problems, but they have to solve them anyway.** People have to change their own behavioral responses and alter their environment for their life to change. 5. New behavior has to be learned in all relevant contexts. New behavioral skills have to be practiced in the situations where the skills are needed, not just in the situation where the skills are first learned. 6. All behaviors (actions, thoughts, emotions) are caused. There is always a cause or set of causes for our actions, thoughts, and emotions, even if we do not know what the causes are. 7. Figuring out and changing the causes of behavior work better than judging and blaming. Judging and blaming are easier, but if we want to create change in the world, we have to change the chains of events that cause unwanted behaviors and events. *But trying harder and being more motivated may not be needed if progress is steady and at a realistic rate of improvement. **Parents and caregivers must assist children in this task.

OurSpace shared-use support notes

Use this as a no-blame map. The point is not who failed; the point is what barrier showed up and what support would make the next try smaller.

Tiny steps

  • Name the moment without blame.
  • Circle one link: body, brain, access, trauma cue, timing, or environment.
  • Choose one repair or prevention step.
  • Save the lesson; do not punish the person.

Flare-day versions

  • Minimum: One breath, one label, one safe message, or one checkbox.
  • Medium: Two to five minutes with everything reachable from bed/chair.
  • Full: Use the original worksheet/handout when both bodies and brains have capacity.

Accessibility modifications

  • Do chain analysis verbally, by voice note, or with one checkbox.
  • Include pain, oxygen, MCAS, fatigue, dissociation, caregiver load, and mobility access as real links.

Relationship and caregiver notes

  • William's needs and Jasper's capacity both count.
  • Ask before helping; ask before escalating; name the smallest next step.
  • Pause means pause, not abandonment. I am coming back. I care about us. I need a smaller next step.
  • What is needed, what capacity exists, what can be smaller, and what can wait?

Avoid or adjust

  • Do not use this as a courtroom, interrogation, or proof that someone did not try.
