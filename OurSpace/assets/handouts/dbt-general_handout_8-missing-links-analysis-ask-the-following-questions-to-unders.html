General Handout 8 (General Worksheet 3) Missing-Links Analysis Ask the following questions to understand how and why effective behavior that is needed or expected did not occur. 1. Did you know what effective behavior was needed or expected (what skills homework was given, what skills to use, etc.)? If no to Question 1, ask what got in the way of knowing what was needed or expected. Ideas might include not paying attention, unclear instructions, never getting the instructions in the first place, becoming too overwhelmed and couldn’t process the information, and so on. Problem-solve what got in the way. For example, you might work on paying attention, ask for clarification when you don’t understand instructions, call others, look up information, and so on. 2. If yes to Question 1, ask were you willing to do the needed or expected effective behavior? If no to Question 2, ask what got in the way of willingness to do effective behaviors. Ideas might include willfulness, feeling inadequate, or feeling demoralized. Problem-solve what got in the way of willingness. For example, you might practice radical acceptance, do pros and cons, practice opposite action, and so on. 3. If yes to Question 2, ask did the thought of doing the needed or expected effective behavior ever enter your mind? If no to Question 3, Problem-solve how to get the thought of doing effective behaviors into your mind. For example, you might put it on your calendar, set your alarm to go off, put your skills notebook next to your bed, practice coping ahead with difficult situations (see Emotion Regulation Handout 19), and so on. 4. If yes to Question 3, ask what got in the way of doing the needed or expected effective behavior right away? Ideas might include putting it off, continuing to procrastinate, not being in the mood, forgetting how to do what was needed, thinking that no one would care anyway (or no one would find out), and so on. Problem-solve what got in the way. For example, you might set a reward for doing what is expected, practice opposite action, do pros and cons, and so on.

OurSpace shared-use support notes

Use this as a no-blame map. The point is not who failed; the point is what barrier showed up and what support would make the next try smaller.

Tiny steps

  • Name the moment without blame.
  • Circle one link: body, brain, access, trauma cue, timing, or environment.
  • Choose one repair or prevention step.
  • Save the lesson; do not punish the person.

Flare-day versions

  • Minimum: One breath, one label, one safe message, or one checkbox.
  • Medium: Two to five minutes with everything reachable from bed/chair.
  • Full: Use the original worksheet/handout when both bodies and brains have capacity.

Accessibility modifications

  • Do chain analysis verbally, by voice note, or with one checkbox.
  • Include pain, oxygen, MCAS, fatigue, dissociation, caregiver load, and mobility access as real links.

Relationship and caregiver notes

  • William's needs and Jasper's capacity both count.
  • Ask before helping; ask before escalating; name the smallest next step.
  • Pause means pause, not abandonment. I am coming back. I care about us. I need a smaller next step.
  • What is needed, what capacity exists, what can be smaller, and what can wait?

Avoid or adjust

  • Do not use this as a courtroom, interrogation, or proof that someone did not try.
