☐ Emotion Regulation Handout 19 (Emotion Regulation Worksheets 12, 13) Build Mastery and Cope Ahead Build Mastery 1. Plan on doing at least one thing each day to build a sense of accomplishment. Example: 2. Plan for success, not failure. •• Do something difficult, but possible. 3. Gradually increase the difficulty over time. •• If the first task is too difficult, do something a little easier next time. 4. Look for a challenge. •• If the task is too easy, try something a little harder next time. Cope Ahead of Time with Difficult Situations 1. Describe the situation that is likely to prompt problem behavior. •• Check the facts. Be specific in describing the situation. •• Name the emotions and actions likely to interfere with using your skills. 2. Decide what coping or problem-solving skills you want to use in the situation. •• Be specific. Write out in detail how you will cope with the situation and with your emotions and action urges. 3. Imagine the situation in your mind as vividly as possible. •• Imagine yourself in the situation now, not watching the situation. 4. Rehearse in your mind coping effectively. •• Rehearse in your mind exactly what you can do to cope effectively. •• Rehearse your actions, your thoughts, what you say, and how to say it. •• Rehearse coping effectively with new problems that come up. •• Rehearse coping effectively with your most feared catastrophe. 5. Practice relaxation after rehearsing.

OurSpace shared-use support notes

Emotion work starts with the body and access needs. Hunger, pain, oxygen, sleep attacks, MCAS reactions, trauma cues, and exhaustion count.

Tiny steps

  • Name the emotion.
  • Check body basics: breathing, pain, food/water, meds, temperature, sensory load.
  • Lower the demand by one size.
  • Choose soothe, solve, pause, or ask for help.

Flare-day versions

  • Minimum: One breath, one label, one safe message, or one checkbox.
  • Medium: Two to five minutes with everything reachable from bed/chair.
  • Full: Use the original worksheet/handout when both bodies and brains have capacity.

Accessibility modifications

  • Use low-exertion regulation first.
  • Allow anger and grief without turning them into shame.
  • Track patterns without moral judgment.

Relationship and caregiver notes

  • William's needs and Jasper's capacity both count.
  • Ask before helping; ask before escalating; name the smallest next step.
  • Pause means pause, not abandonment. I am coming back. I care about us. I need a smaller next step.
  • What is needed, what capacity exists, what can be smaller, and what can wait?

Avoid or adjust

  • Do not demand immediate calm during respiratory distress, pain flares, dissociation, or overload.
