Interpersonal Effectiveness Handout 13 (I. E. Worksheet 10) Ending Relationships A destructive relationship has the quality of destroying or completely spoiling either the quality of the relationship or aspects of yourself—such as your physical body and safety, your self-esteem or sense of integrity, your happiness or peace of mind, or your caring for the other person. An interfering relationship is one that blocks or makes difficult your pursuing goals that are impor- tant to you; your ability to enjoy life and do things you like doing; your relationships with other per- sons; or the welfare of others that you love. Decide to end relationships in Wise Mind, never in emotion mind. If the relationship is important and not destructive, and there is reason to hope it can be improved, try problem solving to repair a difficult relationship. cope ahead to troubleshoot and practice ending the relationship ahead of time. Be direct: Use the DEAR MAN GIVE FAST interpersonal effectiveness skills. Practice opposite action for love when you find you love the wrong person. Practice safety first! Before leaving a highly abusive or life-threatening relationship, call a local domestic violence hotline or the toll-free National Domestic Violence Hotline (1-800-799-7233) for help with safety planning and a referral to a qualified professional. See also the International Directory of Domestic Violence Agencies (www.hotpeachpages.net).

OurSpace shared-use support notes

Use this to protect both people: needs are real, boundaries are kind, and a pause is not abandonment when repair is named.

Tiny steps

  • Name the ask in one sentence.
  • Name capacity honestly.
  • Offer one smaller option.
  • Agree on a return time or next check-in.

Flare-day versions

  • Minimum: One breath, one label, one safe message, or one checkbox.
  • Medium: Two to five minutes with everything reachable from bed/chair.
  • Full: Use the original worksheet/handout when both bodies and brains have capacity.

Accessibility modifications

  • Add reassurance before hard asks.
  • Use text/chat if speech is hard.
  • Include caregiver capacity as part of the plan, not an afterthought.

Relationship and caregiver notes

  • William's needs and Jasper's capacity both count.
  • Ask before helping; ask before escalating; name the smallest next step.
  • Pause means pause, not abandonment. I am coming back. I care about us. I need a smaller next step.
  • What is needed, what capacity exists, what can be smaller, and what can wait?

Avoid or adjust

  • Do not use skills to win, corner, threaten leaving, or make either person prove pain or disability.
