Interpersonal Effectiveness Handout 4 (I. E. Worksheet 3) Clarifying Goals in Interpersonal Situations Objectives Effectiveness: Getting What You Want from Another Person •• Obtaining your legitimate rights. •• Getting another person to do something you want that person to do. •• Saying no to an unwanted or unreasonable request. •• Resolving an interpersonal conflict. •• Getting your opinion or point of view taken seriously. Questions 1. What specific results or changes do I want from this interaction? 2. What do I have to do to get the results? What will work? Relationship Effectiveness: Keeping and Improving the Relationship •• Acting in such a way that the other person keeps liking and respecting you. •• Balancing immediate goals with the good of the long-term relationship. •• Maintaining relationships that matter to you. Questions 1. How do I want the other person to feel about me after the interaction is over (whether or not I get the results or changes I want)? 2. What do I have to do to get (or keep) this relationship? Self-Respect Effectiveness: Keeping or Improving Self-Respect •• Respecting your own values and beliefs. •• Acting in a way that makes you feel moral. •• Acting in a way that makes you feel capable and effective. Questions 1. How do I want to feel about myself after the interaction is over (whether or not I get the results or changes I want)? 2. What do I have to do to feel that way about myself? What will work?

OurSpace shared-use support notes

Use this to protect both people: needs are real, boundaries are kind, and a pause is not abandonment when repair is named.

Tiny steps

  • Name the ask in one sentence.
  • Name capacity honestly.
  • Offer one smaller option.
  • Agree on a return time or next check-in.

Flare-day versions

  • Minimum: One breath, one label, one safe message, or one checkbox.
  • Medium: Two to five minutes with everything reachable from bed/chair.
  • Full: Use the original worksheet/handout when both bodies and brains have capacity.

Accessibility modifications

  • Add reassurance before hard asks.
  • Use text/chat if speech is hard.
  • Include caregiver capacity as part of the plan, not an afterthought.

Relationship and caregiver notes

  • William's needs and Jasper's capacity both count.
  • Ask before helping; ask before escalating; name the smallest next step.
  • Pause means pause, not abandonment. I am coming back. I care about us. I need a smaller next step.
  • What is needed, what capacity exists, what can be smaller, and what can wait?

Avoid or adjust

  • Do not use skills to win, corner, threaten leaving, or make either person prove pain or disability.
