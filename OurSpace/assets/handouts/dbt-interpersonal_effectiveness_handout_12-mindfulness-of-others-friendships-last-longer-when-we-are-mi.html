Interpersonal Effectiveness Handout 12 (I. E. Worksheet 9) Mindfulness of Others Friendships last longer when we are mindful. Observe   Pay attention with interest and curiosity to others around you.   Stop multitasking; focus on the people you are with.   Stay in the present rather than planning what to say next.   Let go of a focus on self, and focus on others around you.   Be open to new information about others.   Notice judgmental thoughts about others, and let them go.   Give up clinging to always being right. Describe   Replace judgmental words with descriptive words.   Avoid assuming or interpreting what other people think about you without checking the facts. (Remember, no one has ever observed another person’s thoughts, motives, intentions, feelings, emotions, desires, or experiences.)   Avoid questioning other people’s motives (unless you have very good reasons to do so).   Give others the benefit of the doubt. Participate   Throw yourself into interactions with others.   Go with the flow, rather than trying to control the flow.   Become one with group activities and conversations.

OurSpace shared-use support notes

Use this to protect both people: needs are real, boundaries are kind, and a pause is not abandonment when repair is named.

Tiny steps

  • Name the ask in one sentence.
  • Name capacity honestly.
  • Offer one smaller option.
  • Agree on a return time or next check-in.

Flare-day versions

  • Minimum: One breath, one label, one safe message, or one checkbox.
  • Medium: Two to five minutes with everything reachable from bed/chair.
  • Full: Use the original worksheet/handout when both bodies and brains have capacity.

Accessibility modifications

  • Add reassurance before hard asks.
  • Use text/chat if speech is hard.
  • Include caregiver capacity as part of the plan, not an afterthought.

Relationship and caregiver notes

  • William's needs and Jasper's capacity both count.
  • Ask before helping; ask before escalating; name the smallest next step.
  • Pause means pause, not abandonment. I am coming back. I care about us. I need a smaller next step.
  • What is needed, what capacity exists, what can be smaller, and what can wait?

Avoid or adjust

  • Do not use skills to win, corner, threaten leaving, or make either person prove pain or disability.
