Interpersonal Effectiveness Handout 9 (I. E. Worksheet 7) (p. 1 of 2) Troubleshooting: When What You Are Doing Isn’t Working Do I have the skills I need? Check out the instructions. Review what has already been tried. •• Do I know how to be skillful in getting what I want? •• Do I know how to say what I want to say? •• Do I follow the skill instructions to the letter? Do I know what I really want in this interaction? Ask: •• Am I undecided about what I really want in this interaction? •• Am I unsure of my priorities? •• Am I having trouble balancing: •• Asking for too much versus too little? •• Saying no to everything versus saying yes to everything? •• Is fear or shame getting in the way of knowing what I really want? Are short-term goals getting in the way of long-term goals? Ask: •• Is “now, now, now” winning out over getting what I really want in the future? •• Is emotion mind controlling what I say and do instead of Wise Mind? (continued on next page) Interpersonal Effectiveness Handout 9 (p. 2 of 2) Are my emotions getting in the way of using my skills? Ask: •• Do I get too upset to use my skills? •• Are my emotions so high that I am over my skills breakdown point? Are worries, assumptions, and myths getting in my way? Ask: •• Are thoughts about bad consequences blocking my action? “They won’t like me,” “She will think I am stupid.” •• Are thoughts about not deserving things getting in my way? “I am such a bad person I don’t deserve this.” •• Am I calling myself names that stop me from doing anything? “I won’t do it right,” “I’ll probably fall apart,” “I’m so stupid.” •• Do I believe myths about interpersonal effectiveness? “If I make a request, this will show that I am a weak person,” “Only wimps have values.” Is the environment more powerful than my skills? Ask: •• Are the people who have what I want or need more powerful than I am? •• Are other people more in control of the situation than I am? •• Will others be threatened if I get what I want? •• Do others have reasons for not liking me if I get what I want? Other ideas:

OurSpace shared-use support notes

Use this to protect both people: needs are real, boundaries are kind, and a pause is not abandonment when repair is named.

Tiny steps

  • Name the ask in one sentence.
  • Name capacity honestly.
  • Offer one smaller option.
  • Agree on a return time or next check-in.

Flare-day versions

  • Minimum: One breath, one label, one safe message, or one checkbox.
  • Medium: Two to five minutes with everything reachable from bed/chair.
  • Full: Use the original worksheet/handout when both bodies and brains have capacity.

Accessibility modifications

  • Add reassurance before hard asks.
  • Use text/chat if speech is hard.
  • Include caregiver capacity as part of the plan, not an afterthought.

Relationship and caregiver notes

  • William's needs and Jasper's capacity both count.
  • Ask before helping; ask before escalating; name the smallest next step.
  • Pause means pause, not abandonment. I am coming back. I care about us. I need a smaller next step.
  • What is needed, what capacity exists, what can be smaller, and what can wait?

Avoid or adjust

  • Do not use skills to win, corner, threaten leaving, or make either person prove pain or disability.
