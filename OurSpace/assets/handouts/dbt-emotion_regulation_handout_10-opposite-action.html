☐ Emotion Regulation Handout 10 (Emotion Regulation Worksheet 7) Opposite Action Use opposite action when your emotions do not fit the facts or when acting on your emotions is not effective. Every emotion has an action urge. Change the emotion by acting opposite to its action urge. Consider these examples: Emotion Action urge Opposite action Fear Run away/avoid Approach/don’t avoid Anger Attack Gently avoid/be a little nice Sadness Withdraw/isolate Get active Shame Hide/avoid Tell the secret to people who will accept it How to Do Opposite Action, Step by Step Step 1. Identify and name the emotion you want to change. Step 2. Check the facts to see if your emotion is justified by the facts. Check also whether the intensity and duration of the emotion fit the facts. (Example: “Irritation” fits the facts when your car is cut in front of; “road rage” does not.) An emotion is justified when your emotion fits the facts. Step 3. Identify and describe your action urges. Step 4. Ask Wise Mind: Is expression or acting on this emotion effective in this situation? If your emotion does not fit the facts or if acting on your emotion is not effective: Step 5. Identify opposite actions to your action urges. Step 6. Act opposite all the way to your action urges. Step 7. Repeat acting opposite to your action urges until your emotion changes.

OurSpace shared-use support notes

Emotion work starts with the body and access needs. Hunger, pain, oxygen, sleep attacks, MCAS reactions, trauma cues, and exhaustion count.

Tiny steps

  • Name the emotion.
  • Check body basics: breathing, pain, food/water, meds, temperature, sensory load.
  • Lower the demand by one size.
  • Choose soothe, solve, pause, or ask for help.

Flare-day versions

  • Minimum: One breath, one label, one safe message, or one checkbox.
  • Medium: Two to five minutes with everything reachable from bed/chair.
  • Full: Use the original worksheet/handout when both bodies and brains have capacity.

Accessibility modifications

  • Use low-exertion regulation first.
  • Allow anger and grief without turning them into shame.
  • Track patterns without moral judgment.

Relationship and caregiver notes

  • William's needs and Jasper's capacity both count.
  • Ask before helping; ask before escalating; name the smallest next step.
  • Pause means pause, not abandonment. I am coming back. I care about us. I need a smaller next step.
  • What is needed, what capacity exists, what can be smaller, and what can wait?

Avoid or adjust

  • Do not demand immediate calm during respiratory distress, pain flares, dissociation, or overload.
